Hi 👋 I’m a second-year undergraduate in Computer Science and Engineering at Seoul National University, and an undergraduate researcher in Prof. Jaesik Park’s lab.

I work on what neural networks actually compute, and how much of that computation is necessary — measuring how information travels through a model, and using those measurements to make inference cheaper without retraining. Before that, most of my research was combinatorial: Markov-chain analysis of group-division strategies, learned heuristics for NP-hard graph problems, and mixed-integer routing.

The through-line is that I like problems where a clean mathematical object — a Markov chain, a kernel, a spectrum — turns something people had been estimating by simulation into something you can compute exactly. I also compete in algorithmic contests, which is where I learned to write code that has to be right the first time.

Research

Inductive Bias of Vision Models via the Metaformer Framework Mar 2026 – present
Undergraduate researcher, 4-person independent research team

Prior comparisons of ViT, local ViT, MLP-Mixer and Convformer report accuracy and stop there. We isolate the token mixer inside a unified Metaformer backbone and ask what each one biases the model toward. I proposed Effective Receptive Distance (ERD), which weights Manhattan distance by each anchor patch’s effective receptive field to quantify how far a model reaches for information, and implemented the ERF and Centered Kernel Alignment pipelines in PyTorch. Findings so far: architectural long-range bias produces large ERD independently of accuracy, data-specific weights pull ERD sharply inward, locality models transform representations in discrete blocks while long-range models do it in one pass, and data-specific weights flatten the loss surface while increasing non-convexity.

Minimum Weighted Vertex Cover via GCN + Guided Local Search 2024

A hybrid heuristic for MWVC: a graph convolutional network proposes candidate covers, then guided local search refines them under weighted-cost constraints. Closed 25% of the optimality gap against greedy and LP-relaxation baselines on benchmark graphs, aimed at large sparse networks where exact solvers are out of reach.

Optimal Group Division for Multi-Person Rock–Paper–Scissors 2023 – 2024
Team leader and lead presenter — Regeneron ISEF 2024, Korean national delegation

Twenty people playing rock–paper–scissors to a single winner takes roughly a thousand rounds. Prior work split players into groups to speed this up but ignored the unfairness that uneven splits create. We defined an efficiency metric (expected rounds) and a fairness metric (spread of win probabilities), and computed the efficiency metric exactly — where prior work could only approximate it — by Jordan-diagonalizing the Markov chain over group states. A 300-line C++ program (backtracking to enumerate sequences, DP for the efficiency index) then found the optimal division for every n ≤ 100. The optimal sequence is 5.76% faster and 71.4% fairer than the random baseline, and 40.1% fairer than the fastest sequence at a 1.16% speed cost. The same structure applies to task splitting in parallel computing and to tournament design.

Mobile EV Charging Station Placement and Routing 2024
Pre-URP

A mixed-integer program for joint depot placement and multi-stop routing, built on a GIS-derived travel-time matrix, minimizing demand-weighted lead time under a budget constraint. Simulation and sensitivity analysis showed an 8–9% lead-time reduction over a static-station baseline.
